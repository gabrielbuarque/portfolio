AI can write the words for you. But if you stop practicing, you may lose the path between an idea and the words on the page.

I notice this risk in myself when I do not feel like writing in Portuguese or English. It is easy to ask AI for a first draft and move straight to editing. Sometimes that is useful. The part I pay attention to is whether I start delegating every chance to practice by default.

For me, using AI well does not mean stopping the learning process. It means knowing where it can speed up execution, continuing to develop the skills I need to judge the result, and questioning bad processes instead of accepting them because they have become routine.

## Faster output is not the same as learning

A result created with AI may look better while the tool is available. That alone does not tell us what someone can do after the tool is removed.

A randomized field experiment published in *PNAS* helps illustrate the difference, with an important limit: the study followed high school students solving math problems at one school in Turkey, not professionals writing at work. During practice, access to GPT-4 improved performance. Later, on an exam without the tool, the group with unrestricted GPT-4 access performed 17% worse than the control group. A tutor configured to give hints instead of answers mitigated that negative effect. ([Bastani et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122))

That study does not prove that AI makes people lose their writing ability. It supports a narrower point: doing better while a tool is available is not the same as learning a skill that remains when the tool is gone. I treat that distinction as a practical warning.

## I learn better when I can experiment

In school and college, I tried to learn in the way I was taught: theory, exams, and a set study format. Those things matter, but I realized that I learn a lot by interacting with things and figuring out what happens underneath.

When I see a share icon, for example, I usually understand that the button will share something. I also like to test what happens when I click, observe the result, and figure out how the system got there. That practical curiosity helps me build a mental model instead of memorizing a sequence.

AI can support that kind of learning when I ask for explanations, examples, questions, or feedback. It can also interrupt it if I delegate the whole reasoning process and accept the first response without trying to understand it. The difference comes from the role I give the tool.

## Frustration only helps when I act on it

I get bothered when a design is confusing, a process is slow, or a way of working continues without anyone asking why. That discomfort can point to an opportunity, but frustration by itself does not improve anything. I need to investigate, try a change, and check whether the result actually got better.

That is where AI can help. It can speed up parts of execution: map a process, explore a solution, create a first draft, or automate a step. I still need to bring judgment about the problem, define the intended outcome, and review the work.

A faster process only matters if it reduces rework, improves delivery, or frees time for something important. Producing more for its own sake is not enough. Sometimes keeping a solution simple is better than optimizing a step that barely costs anything.

## Keep practicing the skills behind your judgment

I use AI to expand what I can get done, but I try to keep practicing the skills I need to review, explain, and improve my own work. When I write, that may mean sketching a few ideas before asking for help. When I use an agent for code or research, it may mean defining tests, checking sources, and understanding the changes before accepting them.

I do not need to do everything manually to keep learning. I need to choose what I delegate with care and stay involved enough to tell whether the answer makes sense. AI can shorten the time between an idea and a first attempt. Learning to judge whether that attempt solved the problem is still part of my work.

In the end, I want to notice what can improve, act on it, and remain able to understand the result. AI speeds up execution. The habit of studying, experimenting, and reviewing helps me decide what is worth doing.
